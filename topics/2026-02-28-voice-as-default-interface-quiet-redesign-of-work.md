# Narrative brief

A 2–3 minute reflective solo episode on a near-future everyday shift: voice isn’t just for dictation; it becomes the primary way people draft, search, and coordinate—changing attention, privacy, and social norms.

Concrete anchors the narrator can land on:
1) A specific moment: walking between meetings (or commuting) and “writing” an email, summarizing a call, and creating a to-do list entirely by voice—then noticing how different it feels versus typing (faster, more fluid, but also less precise and easier to overshare).
2) A social friction point: speaking sensitive work aloud in public spaces (train, café, open office) and the new etiquette that follows—people lowering their voice, stepping outside, or switching to text for confidential details.

Talking points to weave in (keep it tight, not a list):
- Voice changes cognition: you draft in sentences rather than bullet points; it can improve flow but can also hide weak structure.
- Voice makes “micro-work” possible in dead time, but it also risks extending work into every gap.
- Privacy tradeoff: your thoughts become audible; the environment becomes part of your workflow.
- A simple closing question: “What will we protect when our tools make it effortless to speak everything?”

Tone: observant and human, not hype; end with one small practice suggestion (e.g., a personal rule: voice for rough drafts and reflections, keyboard for final decisions and anything sensitive).
